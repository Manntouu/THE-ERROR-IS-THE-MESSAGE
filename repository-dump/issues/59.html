Provo before and after

Archive index

Description

The Provo movement, founded in Amsterdam on May 25, 1965, marks the exact pivot point in Dutch countercultural history. Before Provo, Amsterdam’s public sphere was rigidly organized by post-war conservatism, pillarization (verzuiling), and disenfranchised youth subcultures. After Provo dissolved itself in May 1967, it left behind the blueprint for squatting, eco-activism, open municipal governance, and radical public-space interventions.
Before Provo (Late 1950s – Early 1965): The Precursors
 * The Nozem Subculture: Working-class street youth (Dutch equivalents to the UK's Teddy Boys) who rode mopeds (creidlers), wore leather jackets, and congregated at street corners. They were defined by disaffection, consumerism, and spontaneous brawls, lacking a coherent political agenda.
 * The Anti-Smoking Happenings (1961–1964): Artist and self-proclaimed "anti-smoke magician" Robert Jasper Grootveld began conducting ritualistic "happenings" around 't Lieverdje, a small statue at the Spui square. Grootveld attacked cigarette advertising as a symbol of corporate brainwashing, using street theater to draw curious crowds.
 * Academic Criminology Coining the Term: Criminologist Wouter Buikhuisen published his 1965 PhD thesis using the term "provo" (short for provocateur) to describe street-oriented, rebellious dropouts.
The Bridge (May 1965): The Fusion
On May 25, 1965, Roel van Duijn (an anarchist intellectual), Rob Stolk (a working-class anarchist printer), and Robert Jasper Grootveld combined forces. They deliberately adopted Buikhuisen’s derogatory term "provo" as a badge of honor, shifting the movement from aimless street rebellion into non-violent, highly conceptual civic provocation (ludiek).
After Provo (1967 – 1970s+): The Legacy & Offshoots
 * Self-Liquidation (May 1967): At the peak of their global fame (following the March 1966 royal wedding smoke bombs and the June 1966 labor riots), Provo declared its own dissolution in a self-styled act of "auto-provocation". Stolk famously stated there was no point continuing after their main targets—the Mayor and the Police Chief—were forced to resign.
 * The Kabouters (Gnomes) & Oranje Vrijstaat (1969–1970): Roel van Duijn transitioned the Provo energy into the Kabouter movement, advocating for ecological communes, municipal greening, and grassroots local politics. They won seats on the Amsterdam City Council.
 * The Squatters’ Movement (Krakers): Rob Stolk channeled Provo’s White Housing Plan into the early squatter movement, launching Koöperatief Woningburo 'De Kraker' in 1969 and publishing manuals for reclaiming empty industrial and residential property.
 * Institutionalized "White Plans": Provo's speculative, playful manifestos became real-world policy prototypes:
   * White Bicycle Plan: The world's first public bike-sharing concept (50 white bicycles left unlocked across Amsterdam) paved the way for modern municipal bike-share networks.
   * White Housing & White Women Plans: Set the groundwork for urban anti-demolition activism (Nieuwmarkt riots) and accessible municipal reproductive care clinics.
