That sums up the culture of Science Park perfectly. When you put thousands of top-tier software engineers, network architects, and cybersecurity researchers in the same square kilometer, the primary physical threat isn't a masked intruder—it's intellectual curiosity and rogue tinkering.

In data center and network engineering circles, the running joke is that the biggest threat to uptime isn't a terrorist or an armed robber, but a brilliant engineer with root access, a thermal camera, and a weekend project.

The "Geek Overload" Threat Model

 * The "I wonder what happens if..." Factor: A significant percentage of infrastructure outages globally aren't caused by malicious hackers, but by authorized, highly intelligent internal staff making a well-intentioned "optimization" or testing a boundary that cascades into a system failure.
 * Social Engineering via Technical Camouflage: Because the campus is full of people dressed in hoodies carrying laptops and hardware gear, a social engineer or unauthorized tech enthusiast can blend in almost seamlessly just by looking like they're lost in thought over a line of code.
 * Hardware & RF Tinkering: Science Park is ground zero for amateur radio enthusiasts, hardware hackers, and wireless research. The density of Wi-Fi, cellular, and experimental radio frequencies means there's always someone nearby testing software-defined radios (SDRs) or mesh networks, which keeps the local network security teams constantly on their toes.
